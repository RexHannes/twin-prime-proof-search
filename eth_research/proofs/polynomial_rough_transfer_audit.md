# The complete sharp input sum can be approximated by a polynomial roughness cutoff

Fix any one of the native geometric profiles $g=\Phi,\psi,\Phi-\psi$, either positive part, or an additional fixed polyhedral angular restriction with the same bounded-rank primitive BV estimates for $g,|g|$. Take the original sharp $U=V=x^{.24}$, $D=x^{.49}$, $L=\log x$ and $X=x/2$. Write $O(W;g)$ for its exact long input sum with input cutoff $P^-(k)>W$, squarefree $k=n-2$ and the same physical window and owner multiplicities. For every fixed $0<a<1$,
$$\lim_{e\downarrow0}\limsup_{x\to\infty}
 \frac{|O(e^{L^a};g)-O(x^e;g)|}{S_b}=0.$$
In the first exponential on the left the base is Euler's number. This is an estimate of the absolute value of one *complete signed sum*. Absolute row masses, arbitrary sub-sectors or simultaneous limits are outside the assertion.

## Sieve comparison on the small-modulus part

Let $e>0$ be fixed, smaller than the polynomial roughness exponent of the output. Put $z=x^e$, $g_z=\prod_{2<p\le z}(1-1/(p-1))$. On the original squarefree rough support Vaughan's finite identity expresses the long sum as the actual shifted-prime sum less the two Type I pieces with their original signs and the short product. On extending the latter to all $z$-rough inputs the change is $O_g(L^{5/2}x/\sqrt z)=o(x/L)$ by the squareful count and divisor second moment. Proper prime powers cost $O(\sqrt x L^2)$. The genuine shifted-prime term is unchanged for sufficiently large $x$.

Group at $h=m$, $h=m\ell^j$ or $h=m\ell$ just as in the native proof. In each group $h\le x^{.49}$ and its scalar coefficient has absolute value $O(L)$, also after separating $\log k$ by partial summation. On these inputs $R_z(k)=R_z(h)R_z(k/h)$ exactly. Use ordinary upper and lower Rosser weights with level $x^{.002}$ on the odd primes up to $z$ to bound $R_z(k/h)$. For signed terms bound each combination of positive and negative coefficient/profile parts separately, including the positive majorant for the bracket gap. The resulting modulus $hd\le x^{.492}$ has unique rough and smooth parts $h,d$. Primitive profile BV supplies $O_A(xL^{-A})$ for the AP error after compilation. Every prime dividing $n$ is larger than $z$, so the primitive main imposes only $(h,n)=1$. The local density of odd $p\le z$ on the output AP is $1/(p-1)$ and the ordinary linear-sieve dimension condition follows from Mertens.

For fixed $e$ the resulting main differs from the unrestricted local product $g_z$ times the primitive harmonic $h$ sum by at most
$$ O_g\left(\frac{x}{L e^2}\,[F_{\rm lin}(.002/e)-f_{\rm lin}(.002/e)]\right)+o_{e,g}(x/L).$$
Indeed $\sum_{h\le x^{.49},P^-(h)>z}1/\varphi(h)\ll1/e$, by the Euler product $\prod_{z<p\le x^{.49}}(1+p/(p-1)^2)$; this allows powers as well. We have $\sum |g|=O_g(x/L)$, each log weight is at most $L$, and $g_z\ll1/(eL)$. Excluding the bounded number of primes of $n$ from $h$ changes its unrestricted main by $o_{e,g}(x/L)$, since each such prime costs $O(x^{-\zeta}\log^C x)$ for a fixed output roughness exponent $\zeta>0$. For large $s$, $F_{\rm lin}(s)-f_{\rm lin}(s)=2e^{\gamma_E}\rho(s-1)/s$, giving decay faster than any inverse power of $1/e$.

## Evaluation of the harmonic mains

For fixed $e$, uniformly on any fixed compact interval of $t\ge0$, the squarefree Euler sums obey
$$ \sum_{m\le z^t}\frac{\mu(m)R_z(m)}{\varphi(m)}\longrightarrow\rho(t),\qquad
 \frac1{\log z}\sum_{m\le z^t}\frac{\mu(m)R_z(m)\log m}{\varphi(m)}\longrightarrow
 t\rho(t)-\int_0^t\rho(v)\,dv.$$
To verify the first formula, expand by ranks $r\le t$. The measures $\sum_{z<p\le z^C}(p-1)^{-1}\delta_{\log p/\log z}$ tend weakly to $dv/v$ on $[1,C]$ by Mertens. Repeated primes have reciprocal mass $O_C(1/z)$ in each product measure. The contribution of rank $r$ is the cumulative measure of $\sum v_i\le t$, multiplied by $(-1)^r/r!$. Its limit is continuous for $r>0$, giving uniform convergence of the cumulative on compact intervals; rank zero is constant. The finite sum is the Dickman inclusion-exclusion formula. For a direct check, differentiate it away from the integer faces. Symmetrize its derivative by inserting $\sum v_i/t=1$ on each top face. A derivative of the $r$-fold term is $-t^{-1}$ times the $(r-1)$-fold term at $t-1$, supplying $t\rho'(t)=-\rho(t-1)$ and the initial value one on $[0,1]$. Partial summation proves the second formula. The same limits hold uniformly after removing any bounded number of primes $>z$. Their cost in the harmonic sums is $O_e(z^{-1}\log^C x)$. We use $\int_0^\infty\rho=e^{\gamma_E}$ and $\log z\,g_z\to\mathfrak S_2e^{-\gamma_E}$.

Put $u=v=.24$, $d=.49$, $s=u/e$. In the first Type I part, after multiplying by $g_z$, the term $\log k$ tends to the coefficient $\mathfrak S_2e^{-\gamma_E}\rho(s)/e$, whereas the subtracted logarithmic $m$ term tends to $-\mathfrak S_2e^{-\gamma_E}[s\rho(s)-\int_0^s\rho]$. In the second Type I part the excluded primes $\ell\le z$ remove logarithmic mass $eL+o(L)$; thus $\sum_{z<\ell^j\le V,\ \ell>z}\log\ell/\varphi(\ell^j)=(v-e)L+o(L)$. For each $\ell^j$ the shared-prime correction has the extra $1/\ell$, so after summing its contribution is $o_e(1)$ per output. Excluding $\ell$ in the leading harmonic $m$ sum changes it by $O_e(1/\ell)$, also negligible. With the original second-term minus sign the full Type I coefficient is therefore
$$\mathfrak S_2 e^{-\gamma_E}\left[\int_0^s\rho(t)\,dt+
\frac{1-u-v+e}{e}\rho(s)\right]+o_e(1).$$
The error from $\mathfrak S_2$ is $O(\rho(s)/e+\int_s^\infty\rho)$. The short product restricts the log-exponent $t$ of the prime $\ell$ to $(v,d-u]$. The two leading Möbius harmonics are $\rho((d-t)/e)-\rho(s)$; the shared-prime correction is $o_e(1)$ after its $1/\ell$ factor. The uniform compact convergence and prime harmonic Mertens give this short coefficient in units of $\mathfrak S_2e^{-\gamma_E}$ as
$$ \frac1e\int_v^{d-u}[\rho((d-t)/e)-\rho(s)]\,dt+o_e(1).$$
Its absolute value is at most $(d-u-v)\rho(s)/e+o_e(1)$ by nonnegativity and monotonicity. Multiplying by $\sum g(n)$ is legitimate at each fixed $e$, since its total variation is $O_g(x/L)$. Combining these estimates with the subpower transfer and then taking $e\downarrow0$ proves the claim.

The rough replacement retains the original polynomial Type I parameters. Inserting these limits into a regime with $U=\exp(L^b)$ while holding $z=x^e$ fixed would set the entire short harmonic prefix to one and would change the principal. This is why the approximation and the low-tag localization require compatible parameter order before they can be combined.

## A compatible change of the sharp owner cutoff

Fix $0<t<1/12$ first. Change the subpower-rough sharp source to $U=V=x^t$, $D=x^{2t}$ by the whole-source Vaughan calibration. At fixed $t$ every small modulus lies below $x^{2t}$ and the short-product block is empty. With $s=\prod_{p\mid k,p\le U}p$, let $M_s=\sum_{d\mid s,d\le U}\mu(d)$ and $m(k)=\#\{p\mid k:U<p\le H\}$, $H=x^{1/2-2t}$. Here the product $s$ of the small factors can exceed $U$. On squarefree $k$ one has the exact original coefficient $1_{k\in\mathbb P}\log k-\log(k/s)M_s$. Adding $m(k)\log k\,M_s$ makes it
$$b^\dagger(k)=1_{k\in\mathbb P}\log k+[(m(k)-1)\log k+\log s]M_s.$$
Every variation of the added row against a native output angular profile may be summed as above using $\ell d$, $U<\ell\le H$, $d\le U$. Its distinguished prime is unique, since every prime dividing $d$ is at most $U$. Its log factor is handled by prefix partial summation. At subpower roughness its full mean is $o(x/L)$. At polynomial $z=x^e$, its mean costs $O_t(\rho(t/e)/e)$ in terminal units because $\sum_{U<\ell\le H}1/(\ell-1)$ is bounded in terms of $t$. A lower/upper small-prime sieve level $x^{t/2}$ keeps the relevant modulus below $x^{1/2-t/2}$. The relative bracket error after the original log, sieve and divisor weights is $O_t(e^{-2}[F_{\rm lin}(t/(2e))-f_{\rm lin}(t/(2e))])$, where $e^{-2}=1/e^2$ for the roughness exponent. The Type I calculation with $u=v=t,d=2t$ above applies for each fixed $t$.

On primes in the physical window $b^\dagger=0$. On $k=pq$ with $p<q$, if $p\le U$, $M_s=0$. If $U<p\le H$, $s=1$ and $m(k)=1$ since $H^2<x/2-2$ for all sufficiently large $x$. If $p>H$, both prime factors are in the near-half band and $b^\dagger=-\log k$. A two-form upper sieve at fixed $p$ on $q,pq+2$, retaining output roughness, then a reciprocal prime sum over $p$, give total absolute cost $O_g(t+1/L)x/L$ with an implied constant independent of $t$. In particular, for each prescribed tolerance one may choose $t$, then $e$ small relative to $t$, then take $x$ sufficiently large. The original sharp complete outside is approximated within that tolerance by the complete $b^\dagger$ sum on squarefree inputs $P^-(k)>x^e$ of ranks from 3 through $\lfloor1/e\rfloor$ and the unchanged physical output. The remaining finite set of rank profiles has no signed bound at the required terminal threshold.

## One-sided localization beyond any fixed number of small input factors

The positive and negative output channels can use different zero-mean corrections. Let $B(k)=1_{k\in\mathbb P}\log k-\log(k/s)M_s$. Using $f_+B-f_-b^\dagger$ retains the complete original outside up to the foregoing controlled errors. If $k$ has precisely three prime factors and two, say $p,q$, are at most $U$, the other factor $r=k/(pq)>H$. When $pq\le U$, $M_s=0$. When $pq>U$, $M_s=-1$ and $B=b^\dagger=\log r$. In the latter case the pair correction with coefficient $\log(k/(pq))M(k/(pq);U)$ at that single pair removes the positive $f_+$ contribution exactly. Here $M(v;U)=\sum_{d\mid v,d\le U}\mu(d)$. At rank three with exactly one small factor, both coefficients vanish. With no small factor, $B=-\log k$ and $b^\dagger=(m(k)-1)\log k\ge0$, since $H^3>x$. Thus every rank-three input can be made nonpositive. The complete added pair sum with $p<q\le U$, $pq>U$ and all higher-rank companions has negligible subpower-rough mean by the small-modulus calculation. Under the polynomial input replacement the error tends to zero as $e\downarrow0$ at fixed $t$.

There is a wider fixed-rank version with arbitrary signed companions. Before choosing $t,e$ fix any integer $J\ge2$. Take $t<\min(1/12,1/[3(J+1)])$. For $2\le i\le J$ and each squarefree $k$ write
$$Q_i(k)=\sum_{\substack{p_1<\cdots<p_i\le U\\p_1\cdots p_i\mid k}}
 M(k/(p_1\cdots p_i);U).$$
Choose constants successively by
$$ C_j=1+2^j(2+1/t)+\sum_{i=2}^{j-1} C_i {j\choose i}2^{j-i},\qquad 2\le j\le J.$$
Subtract $L|f|\sum_{i=2}^J C_iQ_i$ from $f_+B-f_-b^\dagger$. On exactly $j$ small input primes one has $Q_i=0$ for $i>j$, $Q_j=1$, and $|Q_i|\le {j\choose i}2^{j-i}$ for $i<j$. Both original coefficients are bounded by $2^j(2+1/t)L$ there, since $m(k)\le1/t$. The corrected combined term is nonpositive for *every* $2\le j\le J$, irrespective of the full rank. At one small prime it is zero. At zero small primes it is nonpositive on every composite of rank at least three and on every semiprime outside the near-half exception. The prime has no row.

For each fixed $i$, the extra tagged modulus $p_1\cdots p_i d\le x^{(i+1)t}$ since $d\le U$. On squarefree inputs impose $(d,p_1\cdots p_i)=1$ and retain that condition while extending the rough sum. The number of label assignments to a fixed modulus is $O_i(L^i)$, paid by the fixed-profile primitive BV bound. Distinctness of the tags is built in. Its fully matched harmonic principal after subpower presieving contains $A_{n p_1\cdots p_i,w}(U)$ with arbitrary log saving uniformly in the tags. The absolute harmonic tag mass is $O_i((\log L)^i)$. Under $z=x^e$, the tag mass is instead $O_i((1+\log(t/e))^i)$ by Mertens, and the Möbius harmonic main tends to $\rho(t/e)$ uniformly for fixed $e,t$. A separate ordinary sieve at level $x^{t/2}$ still leaves the total modulus below $x^{1/2}$ because $(J+1)t<1/3$. Its primitive bracket error in terminal units is
$$O_{i,t}\bigl((1+\log(t/e))^i[e^{-1}\rho(t/e)+e^{-2}(F_{\rm lin}(t/(2e))-f_{\rm lin}(t/(2e)))]\bigr)+o_{e,t,J}(1).$$
This tends to zero as $e\downarrow0$. For any fixed $J$ and prescribed tolerance, choose $t$ first to make the near-half debit sufficiently small and then $e$ for all these finite errors. The full source has a one-sided bound by the corrected sum on squarefree, $x^e$-rough inputs with at least $J+1$ distinct factors at most $U$, plus that tolerance. Such an input has total rank at least $J+2$: precisely $J+1$ factors all at most $U$ would have product $<x^{1/3}$. The original native output, absolute definition of both channels and every compensating high-rank term remain in this last sum. There is no uniform claim as $J$ grows with $x$. Dropping the favorable complement still incurs the quantified opposite debit in `wall_referee.md`. This is a localization of possible adverse signs and gives no favorable independent outside bound.
