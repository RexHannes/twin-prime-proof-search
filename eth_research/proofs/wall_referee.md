## One low tag

Use $F(y)=\mathbb P(U<y)$ and $M(r)=\sum_{d\mid r}\mu(d)(1-F(d))$. The low compensator satisfies the exact identity
$$W(p,k)\log p=(1-F(p))\log p+F(p)\log k.$$
After averaging sharp cutoffs in $M$, the grouped modulus is $pd\le x^{1/2-c}$ at the polynomial parameter values. Folding the input subpower sieve preserves a fixed power gap. With squarefree $k$ one may also explicitly impose $(p,d)=1$ before extending the sum. Its principal is then exactly $g_w A_{np,w}(U)/(p-1)$; the alternate convention that permits $p\mid d$ introduces the independently checked extra multiple $-p^{-1}A_{np,w}(U/p)$ inside the bracket. The absolute extension to squareful $w$-rough inputs costs $O_A(xL^{-A})$ either way. The bounded variation of the output factor $\log k$ and arbitrary harmonic Möbius savings kill the signed principal. This proves the low-tag cancellation only on the complete input sum for the specified output test. Every prime input $k$ in the window exceeds $H$, and its original large coefficient is zero.

For squarefree composite $k$ with no prime $\le T=x^{2c}$, each prefix is one. Consequently $B=-\log k$ and $B'=(j-1)\log k$, with $j=\#\{p\mid k:p\le H\}$. At rank at least three $j\ge1$. On exactly one small prime $s\le T$, if $s\le V$ every row vanishes. If $s>V$, then $B=-\log s-F(s)\log(k/s)$ and $B'=F(s)j'\log k$, where $j'$ counts the other low tags. These identities verify all signs. Two owners both exceeding $H=x^{1/2-3c}$ give an $O(c)x/L$ total by the two-linear-form upper sieve and reciprocal mass in the near-half band. The analogous subpower choice is $V=e^{L^b}$, $T=e^{2L^b}$, $w=e^{L^a}$ with $0<a<b<1$ and $H=x^{1/2-\eta}$ at fixed $\eta>0$. The same exceptional bound is $O(\eta)x/L$ uniformly in $\eta$ after fixing it. For the native Möbius partial sums the shortest argument $U\ge e^{L^b}$ still pays all log powers. The smooth-prime Rankin tail is exponentially small in $L^{b-a}$.

An explicit adverse sign remains on the input profile $k=pst$, where $s,t\le V$, $F(st)>0$ and $p>x^{1/2}$. Both small primes are ineligible owners. The large prime's prefix is $-F(st)$, giving $B=B'=F(st)\log p>0$. A probability density compactly supported in $(1,2)$ has a threshold $d_0<2$ with $F(x^{c d_0})>0$ in the power case. Choose $d_0/2<u_1<u_2<1$ and both $s,t\in[x^{cu_1},x^{cu_2}]$. The unordered double reciprocal mass tends to $\tfrac12\log^2(u_2/u_1)>0$ at every fixed $c$, independently of $c$. This angular sign calculation supplies no lower density for a shifted pair of specified input and output profiles.

## A quantified loss if the favorable zero-small sector is dropped

This issue persists even without proving a joint prime population on the preceding $pst$ example. Put $P=\Pi(f_+)>0$; two explicit disjoint native chambers give $P\ge400/566181+10^{-7}$ in `full_certificate_comparison.md`. With the subpower choice above let $N_0$ be the nonnegative sum of $(\log k)f_+(n)$ over all squarefree composite inputs with $P^-(k)>T$. Then
$$\frac{N_0}{S_b}=\left(\frac{e^{-\gamma_E}}{2}P+o(1)\right)L^{1-b}.$$

Indeed fixed-profile BV and Bonferroni of order $C\log L$ give $\sum_n f_+(n)R_T(k)=g_T\sum_n f_+(n)+O_A(xL^{-A})$ at $T=e^{2L^b}$. Every output factor exceeds the polynomial cutoff, so primitive exclusions by the input sieving primes are vacuous. The folded moduli are $x^{o(1)}$, including the positive next-order remainder. Mertens gives $g_T\sim\mathfrak S_2e^{-\gamma_E}/\log T$. Squareful deletions cost $O_f(Lx/T)$. Prime inputs cost $A(f_+)\le(10/3+o(1))P S_b$, by the full nonnegative source estimate. The endpoint rounding is absorbed by $\log k=L+O(1)$. These establish the displayed asymptotic.

On every complementary favorable sector except the near-half semiprime exception the entire signed expression $f_+B-f_-B'$ is nonpositive. In particular the zero-small cohort supplies a debit of at least $N_0$, up to the near-half loss. The full original outside divided by $S_b$ stays bounded on both sides by the arithmetic comparison of its two physical signs, since both have finite mass. Thus the signed residual after dropping all favorable sectors has lower bound $(e^{-\gamma_E}P/2+o(1))L^{1-b}S_b$ when all inputs with at least two small factors are retained. It cannot furnish a finite cap by itself. The debit from the favorable sector must be retained in any eventual coupled bound.

There is a corresponding fixed-power obstruction in the ordered limits. For $T=x^{2c}$ and $c$ sufficiently small relative to the fixed output rough exponent, the ordinary lower dimension-one sieve at level $D=x^{.49}$ applies to $f_+(n)$ with local roots $1/(p-1)$ for odd $p\le T$. Fixed-profile BV pays its remainders. Consequently
$$\liminf_{x\to\infty} \frac{N_0}{S_b}\ge \frac{e^{-\gamma_E}P}{2c} f_{\rm lin}(.49/(2c)) - \frac{10}{3}P.$$
Here $f_{\rm lin}(s)\to1$ as $s\to\infty$. The signed residual obtained by dropping the favorable sectors therefore incurs a lower cost asymptotic to at least $e^{-\gamma_E}P/(2c)$ in the stated ordered limits. This argument also applies to any additional fixed nonnegative output restriction of positive angular mass. If that mass vanishes, this particular obstruction has zero coefficient.

## Eliminating the isolated two-small sign by a further signed correction

The following auxiliary identity holds for either regime above. Write $e=st$ with distinct $w<s<t\le T$. Define, on squarefree inputs,
$$Q_2(k)=\sum_{s<t,\,st\mid k} M(k/(st)).$$
Then for any fixed-profile geometric output $g$ and any fixed $D_0$, the complete mean $\sum_n g(n)R_w(k)\mu^2(k)L^{D_0}Q_2(k)$ is $O_{A,D_0}(xL^{-A})$ for any fixed $A$. For the polynomial regime take $c<1/400$ as in the original packet. For the subpower regime fix $0<a<b<1$.

On squarefree inputs explicitly impose $(d,e)=1$ in the prefix and extend to all rough inputs. Its difference costs at most $O_{D_0}(L^{D_0+2}\sum_{k\text{ squareful},R_w(k)}\tau(k))=O_{A,D_0}(xL^{-A})$. The folded rough modulus is $std\le x^{6c}$ in the first regime and $\le e^{6L^b}$ in the second. For a given rough modulus its number of labeled pairs is at most $O(L^2)$. The subpower Bonferroni divisor is recovered uniquely from the full modulus. The positive remainder and the scalar signed coefficients are paid by primitive BV with deeper log saving. For each rough $e$ the matched principal is $g_w A_{ne,w}(U)/\varphi(e)$ after averaging $U$. The sum of $1/\varphi(e)$ over all pairs is $O((\log L)^2)$. All inserted exclusions are rough, and there are only two extra primes, so the uniform harmonic Möbius estimate finishes the proof. This construction does not require an independent distribution theorem for primes restricted to the $pst$ input class.

There is no tag when a squarefree input has fewer than two small primes. If exactly two occur, $Q_2(k)=1$, because all other factors exceed every permitted $U$. At two small primes $|B|\le4L$ and $|B'|\le 4L(2+L/\log V)$, directly from $|M|\le4$ and $\sum_{p\mid k}\log p\le L$. Therefore subtracting $20L^2 |f(n)| Q_2(k)$ with its full signed higher-rank companions makes the contribution nonpositive on every exactly-two-small input. The mean change for the whole source is $o(x/L)$ by the preceding identity with $g=|f|$. A polynomially bounded coefficient is intentionally used here. The residual on inputs with three or more small primes can be large and must be kept with all its signs.

For each fixed $m$ the same construction with $m$ tags uses $\binom{\omega(q)}m=O_m(L^m)$ representations, a numerator with an arbitrary fixed power of $L$, and level $e^{(2m+2)L^b}$ in the subpower setting. Thus for each *fixed* $J$ one can successively make every input with $2\le j\le J$ small primes nonpositive, by subtracting an $L^{O_J(1)}|f|$ multiple of the $j$-tag kernel. At exactly $j$ small primes this kernel is one and all lower-order tag kernels have absolute size bounded by $\binom ji 2^{j-i}$, so a sufficiently large coefficient exists independent of $x$ apart from its fixed log power. On fewer small primes the new kernel is zero. All modified coefficients and all populations of larger rank belong to the same complete signed identity. No uniform estimate in $J$, no improvement to the best complete upper, and no positive lower bound for the genuine twin-prime count follows. The displayed divergent debit on the zero-small sector is untouched by every one of these higher-tag corrections.
